Auto-frac (self-induced fracturing) is the growth of a man-made hydraulic fracture at an injection well during waterflooding. The simulator models the growth of such a fracture in a poroelastic reservoir, taking the operation of the surrounding wells into account.

The coupled problem is split into a poroelastic reservoir subproblem and an elastic fracture subproblem, and the 3D formulation for a planar fracture is reduced to two-dimensional models: plane strain at early times and a constant-height fracture at late times (Geoenergy Science and Engineering, 2023). The reservoir is solved by the finite element method for the Biot equations and the fracture opening by the displacement discontinuity method; curvilinear fracture growth is modelled by the extended finite element method with the maximum tensile stress criterion.

The auto-frac models were applied to fracture merging in line-drive patterns and to well test interpretation with auto-frac fractures (Neftyanoe Khozyaystvo, 2022 and 2023). The work was supported by the Russian President's scholarship SP-1703.2022.1 (2022–2024).
